# Which ETF Performed Best? A Data Analytics Project

## Research question
Which ETF is the best, based on its performance over the past few years?

## Short answer
There is no single best ETF; it depends on how much risk you accept. Using data as of **8 October 2026** for 50 of the largest US-listed ETFs:

- **Highest 5-year return overall:** semiconductor and tech sector funds, with SMH at about **37.5% a year** and SOXX at about **31.7%**. They are concentrated and have higher fees.
- **Best among broad US funds:** Nasdaq-100 funds (QQQM, QQQ) at about **16.4% a year**, versus about **13.6%** for S&P 500 funds.
- **S&P 500 funds are nearly identical** (VOO, IVV, SPY, SPYM), so fees decide: SPYM 0.02%, VOO and IVV 0.03%, SPY 0.09%.
- **Higher returns came with deeper drawdowns:** QQQ fell about 35% at its worst in the last five years, versus about 25% for VOO.
- **Bond funds lost money** over five years (BND and AGG about −2.2% cumulatively).

## What "best" means here
Raw return favors risky funds, and comparing a bond fund to a semiconductor fund is not meaningful. So results are shown **within category** and next to fees and drawdown data. The main metric is the **5-year annualized total return** (dividends reinvested, already net of fund fees).

## Data elements identified
- **Identification:** ticker, ETF name, issuer, category, benchmark, inception date
- **Price and returns:** daily prices, adjusted close, dividends, 1/3/5-year returns
- **Risk:** volatility, maximum drawdown, beta
- **Risk-adjusted performance:** Sharpe ratio, Sortino ratio, alpha
- **Cost and efficiency:** expense ratio, tracking error, tracking difference, premium/discount to NAV
- **Size and liquidity:** assets under management, trading volume
- **Composition:** top holdings, sector and country weights
- **Market context:** benchmark index, risk-free rate (US 3-month T-bill)

## Where to find the data
- Prices and dividends: [Yahoo Finance via `yfinance`](https://github.com/vinaykale64/yfinance), [OpenBB Platform](https://docs.openbb.co/odp/python/reference/etf/historical)
- Risk-free rate: [FRED series DTB3](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DTB3)
- Fees, AUM, holdings, tracking error: [Trackinsight](https://www.trackinsight.com/en/fund/JUST), [ETF Central](https://www.etfcentral.com/compare-etfs/JUST-vs-VOO)
- Sample datasets: Kaggle's Huge Stock Market Dataset (2017, too old for this window) and a 10-year country-ETF dataset ([TIB](https://service.tib.eu/ldmservice/dataset/etf-data))

## Data used in this analysis
- [chartrow.com – Biggest ETFs](https://chartrow.com/lists/biggest-etfs) (AUM, fees, 1Y and 5Y returns, as of 8 Oct 2026)
- [totalrealreturns.com](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/QQQ,VOO,VOOG,VT,VTI,VXUS) (multi-year returns and drawdowns, through 8 Oct 2026)
- [PortfoliosLab](https://portfolioslab.com/tools/stock-comparison/VOOG/QQQM) (1-year Sharpe and volatility)
- [Motley Fool – VOO vs QQQ](https://www.fool.com/coverage/etfs/2026/07/07/voo-vs-qqq-which-index-fund-is-the-better-buy-for-long-term-investors/) (VOO 5-year drawdown)

## Method
5-year annualized return = (1 + 5-year cumulative total return)^(1/5) − 1. ETFs are ranked overall and within category. Categories were assigned by the authors.

## Limitations
- Volatility, Sharpe and Sortino ratios were not available for most of the 50 ETFs. A full risk-adjusted ranking needs daily prices over the same period.
- Sources sometimes disagree. For example, VOO's size appears as $1.01T on one site and $1.7T on another (which may count all share classes); the lower figure was used.
- Source returns are rounded to 0.1%, so annualized figures may be off by about 0.1 percentage point.
- The list covers the largest US-listed ETFs, so it favors established funds.
- Past performance does not predict future returns. This project is for educational purposes and is not financial advice.
